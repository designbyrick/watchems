Here's the short version: a watch fits when its case suits the size of your wrist and its lug-to-lug (the length of the watch from top to bottom) fits across the top of it. Get those two right and almost any style will look good on you.

It sounds technical, but it really isn't. All you need is a strip of paper, a ruler and about five minutes.

## How to measure your wrist for a watch

Wrap a strip of paper around your wrist, just below the bone, and mark where it overlaps. Lay it flat on a ruler and there it is: your wrist size.

Then measure across. Look down at the back of your wrist and hold the ruler over the flat part, from where it starts to curve down on one side to the same spot on the other. That's your flat width, the actual runway a watch gets to sit on.

<Figure src="/posts/watch-size-guide/measure-wrist.svg" alt="Diagram of a wrist slice: a dashed loop around it for wrist size, and a line across the flat top for flat width" caption="A slice through the wrist, back of the hand up. Wrist size goes all the way around. Flat width is just the top, where the watch sits." />

Two quick tips. Measure the wrist you'll actually wear the watch on, and do it in the evening, when wrists run a little bigger. And if the flat part is hard to pin down, a third of your wrist size gets you close enough to start.

The last column matters more than the middle one. A watch can be the right size on paper and still hang off your wrist if it's long, and that's where most fit problems come from.

## What case diameter tells you, and what it doesn't

Case diameter is the width of the case from side to side, and it's the number you'll see in almost every product name. A Rolex Submariner is 41 mm, for example, and a Tudor Black Bay 58 is 39 mm.

It's a decent clue to how big the face will look. What it won't tell you is how long the watch is from top to bottom, and that's the direction where a watch can run off the edges of your wrist.

## What is lug to lug?

The lugs (the four little arms that hold the strap to the case) add length the diameter ignores. Lug-to-lug measures the whole thing, tip to tip.

The idea is simple. Shorter than your flat width, and the watch sits neatly on top while the strap wraps close. Longer, and the ends stick out past the edges, the strap lifts off your skin, and suddenly the watch looks a size too big.

## What is lug width?

Lug width is the gap between the lugs where the strap goes. It's the number you need when you shop for a new strap: the Laco takes an 18 mm one and the Hamilton field watch takes 20 mm.

It nudges the look, too. A wide strap covers more of your wrist, so a watch with wide lugs reads a bit bigger than its case size.

## Why two watches the same size can look different

Put two watches with the same case size side by side and they can look surprisingly different. A few details do most of the work.

<Figure src="/posts/watch-size-guide/bezel-size.svg" alt="Two watch faces with the same case size: one with a thin bezel and a big dial, one with a wide dark bezel and a smaller dial" caption="Same case, two bezels. A thin ring around the dial leaves more face, so the watch looks bigger. A wide one shrinks the face." />

- **Bezel and dial.** The bezel (the ring around the dial) frames the face. A thin one leaves room for a big dial, so the watch looks larger. A wide one, like the black ring on a Submariner, makes the face look smaller than the case really is.
- **Dial color.** Light dials look bigger than dark ones. Stuck between two sizes? A dark dial is the safer bet.
- **Case shape.** Rectangles play by their own rules. A Cartier Tank Must is listed as 33.7 mm by 25.5 mm, and it's the longer number you check against your flat width.
- **Bracelet or strap.** Metal adds weight and bulk. The same Laco Augsburg 39 weighs 81.5 g on leather and 140 g on its steel bracelet.

## Dress, field, dive: how the type of watch changes the size

Each type of watch has its comfort zone, and the biggest difference between them is often thickness, not width.

- **Dress watches** are small and thin so they slide under a cuff. The Tank Must is just 6.60 mm thick.
- **Field watches** sit in the middle. The Hamilton Khaki Field Mechanical is 38 mm across and 9.5 mm thick.
- **Pilot watches** tend to run a little bigger for easy reading, like the 39 mm Laco Augsburg.
- **Dive watches** are the chunky ones. Hamilton's Khaki Navy Scuba Auto GMT (GMT means it shows a second time zone) is 13.9 mm thick, with a 52 mm lug-to-lug.

As for men's, women's and unisex labels, take them lightly. They mostly describe habit. A small watch on a big wrist can look sharp, and a bigger one on a slim wrist works if it's short enough from tip to tip.

Got a small wrist and a soft spot for dive watches? Skip the giants and hunt for a short lug-to-lug instead. That's a big part of the appeal of a 39 mm diver like the Black Bay 58.

## Test the fit at home with a paper strip

No watch in hand? No problem. Grab the lug-to-lug and the lug width from the spec sheet and make a paper stand-in.

1. Cut a strip of paper as wide as the lug width and as long as the lug-to-lug. For the Hamilton Khaki Field Mechanical, that's 20 mm by 47 mm.
2. Draw a circle in the middle, as wide as the case.
3. Lay it across the top of your wrist, centered, and check it from above and in a mirror.
4. If the ends run past the edges of your wrist, the watch will hang over. If you can see a bit of skin on each side, you're good.

<Figure src="/posts/watch-size-guide/paper-strip.webp" alt="Scissors cutting a narrow paper strip along pencil lines next to a ruler and a plain watch" />

Five minutes, and it works for any watch that lists its lug-to-lug. If you get to try the real thing, wear it around the house for an evening before you decide. Comfort shows up over a few hours, not in the mirror.

<Note title="Common mistake: trusting the number in the name">A watch sold as the smaller size can easily be longer than a bigger one, just like the Laco and the Hamilton above. Check the lug-to-lug before you buy. Not listed? Ask the seller, or look at owner photos.</Note>

## So how big is too big?

There's no magic cutoff. Put the watch on, look down, and watch for three things:

- The lugs reach past the edges of your wrist.
- There's a gap between the strap and your skin right next to the case.
- The watch keeps sliding to one side when your arm hangs down.

Thickness matters too, especially with shirts. A thin watch slips under a cuff, a thick one catches on it. The Casio F-91W is only 8.5 mm thick, one reason it disappears on the wrist, while a 13.9 mm dive watch will let your sleeve know it's there.
